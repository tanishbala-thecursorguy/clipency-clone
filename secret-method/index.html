The Rule of Seven

Once is noise.
Seven times is a decision.

It’s one of the oldest rules in marketing, born in the Hollywood studios of the 1930s: a buyer needs to come across a product around seven times before they act.

Psychologists call the engine behind it the mere-exposure effect. The more often the brain sees something, the more familiar it feels. Familiar feels safe, safe feels trustworthy, and trustworthy gets bought.

Most marketing ignores this. It spends everything on a single moment and hopes. We engineer all seven.

The Seven Touches

What happens in your buyer’s head,
one scroll at a time.

Each touch is placed on purpose: a different page, a different face, a different angle. To the buyer it feels like the world discovering you. To us, it’s a schedule.

  1. 1

    The launch film

    A founder-grade story lands on their feed. They watch three seconds and keep scrolling.

    “Huh. Never heard of them.”
    Curiosity
  2. 2

    A clip on a theme page

    The best moment of that film, cut short, on a page they already follow.

    “Wait, I’ve seen this before.”
    Recognition
  3. 3

    Another clip, another page

    A different cut in a different niche. Now the brain starts filing you under “known”.

    “These guys are everywhere.”
    Familiarity
  4. 4

    A real person using it

    A creator on a page dedicated to your product shows it working in their own hands.

    “Someone like me actually uses it.”
    Social proof
  5. 5

    Another creator, another angle

    A second face, a different use case. It’s no longer one person’s opinion.

    “Okay, people genuinely like this.”
    Validation
  6. 6

    Problem → solution

    The exact pain they live with, then the moment it disappears. They picture it in their own life.

    “That’s literally my problem.”
    Desire
  7. 7

    The product, one more time

    The launch film comes back around. This time they don’t scroll. They search for you.

    “Where do I sign up?”
    Decision
The Levers We Pull

Six principles of the human mind,
pulled at scale.

None of this is luck. Every launch is built on how people actually decide, then repeated thousands of times.

LEVER 01

Mere exposure

The brain prefers what it has seen before. Familiarity is the shortest path to trust.

How we pull it: 1,000–10,000 reposts so the same buyer meets you again and again.
LEVER 02

The frequency illusion

Once you notice something, you start seeing it everywhere, and “everywhere” reads as “popular”.

How we pull it: different pages and niches, so you show up across their whole feed.
LEVER 03

Social proof

People copy people. A stranger’s honest demo beats any brand claim.

How we pull it: 1,000–5,000 UGC videos a month from real faces on dedicated pages.
LEVER 04

Story over specs

Nobody remembers a feature list. Everybody remembers a founder, a pain and a turning point.

How we pull it: six launch films that tell the team, customer, niche, company, problem and product.
LEVER 05

Native beats ads

Feeds have trained people to skip anything that looks like an ad. Content they actually watch.

How we pull it: your launch runs as content on real pages, not in an ad slot they’ve learned to ignore.
LEVER 06

Momentum

A product that feels like it’s taking off pulls people in before they’ve even tried it.

How we pull it: posts keep landing week after week, so the buzz never cools down.
